01   Business Challenge & Project Scope
A fictional technology company wants to automate the employee lifecycle: receiving job applications, reviewing candidate evidence, onboarding new hires, answering policy questions, generating employment documents, and monitoring HR operations. Today, these processes are slow, fragmented, and heavily manual.
Your mission is to design and build an integrated, working HR automation solution using n8n, AI agents, document extraction/OCR, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) knowledge base, and connected business tools.
PROJECT OUTCOME
Show one connected business journey from applicant submission to reviewed hiring, employee onboarding, employee self-service, authorized certificate issuance, and routine HR reporting. The system must have clear human approval points and safe error handling.

Required design principles
•  Use n8n as the primary orchestration platform; a standalone full-stack website is not required.
•  Make each agent responsible for one clear task; ordinary parsing, validation, and PDF export may remain deterministic nodes.
•  Never allow an LLM alone to hire/reject a person, approve a leave request, or authorize an official document.
•  Use fictional people and synthetic documents. The system is an educational prototype, not a production HR platform.

Shared state and handoff requirements
•  Each request has a stable request_id, actor_role, request_type, status, owner, next_action, and audit timestamp.

DESIGN CONSTRAINT
The system must distinguish AI interpretation from business authorization. Agents may recommend a next step; only rules and authorized HR staff may complete restricted actions.
03   Core Workflows — Recruitment
WORKFLOW 1   |   CV Submission & AI Extraction
A candidate uploads a resume to apply for a published role. The system captures the file, extracts usable information, and creates an application record.
•  Create an n8n Form with candidate name, email, target job, and CV upload (PDF or DOCX).
•  Check file type/size, assign a unique application_id, and store the original file in Drive.
•  Extract text from normal PDFs and DOCX files using a suitable parser or converter; use OCR or Vision AI for image-only CVs.
•  Produce validated JSON fields: contact details, education, skills, employment history, and concise summary.
•  Write the record to Candidates Sheet and notify HR once, including links to the original CV and extracted summary.
ACCEPTANCE CRITERION
One valid upload creates one traceable candidate record and one HR notification; uncertain or absent information is clearly flagged.

Failure path: Unsupported or unreadable files must be routed to manual review instead of being silently accepted.
WORKFLOW 2   |   Recruitment Review & Pipeline
HR compares a candidate profile against a specific job description, reviews evidence, and controls each hiring status change.
•  Create at least three fictional job descriptions with explicit technical requirements.
•  Identify matching and missing skills with source evidence from the CV and job description.
•  Present a concise skill-gap summary for HR review; do not generate automated rejection or final hiring decisions.
•  Maintain trackable stages: Applied → Under Review → Interview → Offer → Hired / Closed.
•  After HR updates the status, send the correct notification and log the change, reviewer, and timestamp.
ACCEPTANCE CRITERION
The pipeline can progress only through an explicitly recorded HR action. Every comparison is explainable and does not use protected characteristics.

Failure path: Duplicate applications for the same candidate and job must be detected or flagged; notifications should not repeat unnecessarily.
04   Core Workflow — Employee Onboarding
WORKFLOW 3   |   Onboarding & Document OCR
An approved candidate becomes an employee only after the HR team records the hiring decision. The system opens an onboarding checklist and processes the required employee documents.
•  On HR-confirmed Hired status, assign or locate a unique employee_id and create an employee record.
•  Send an internal onboarding request or form; accept a synthetic national-ID mockup, sample contract, and sample qualification document.
•  Use looping to process several attachments; extract text with file parsing, OCR, or a vision model as appropriate.
•  Reconcile extracted fields with the stored employee profile and flag conflicting or unreadable data.
•  Track each document as Received, Missing, or Needs Review; calculate onboarding completeness from those explicit states.
•  Send a welcome message when appropriate and alert HR about unresolved documents.
ACCEPTANCE CRITERION
For an HR-approved hire, exactly one employee record is created and document statuses are populated. Missing, inconsistent, or blurry documents remain reviewable.

Failure path: A candidate must never be auto-hired by AI; a scan must never be treated as proof that an identity is authentic.

05   Core Workflow — HR Policy Assistant
WORKFLOW 4   |   Retrieval-Augmented HR Self-Service
Employees ask questions about company procedures. The system responds from a versioned, fictional HR knowledge base—not from the LLM’s general assumptions.
•  Prepare at least four policy documents: General HR, Leave, Attendance, and Experience Certificate Policies.
•  Build a document ingestion path with parsing, chunking, embeddings, and vector storage.
•  Retrieve relevant policy passages when a question arrives; return a short answer with verifiable citations (source filename, section, and document version where available).
•  When support is missing or contradictory, explain the limitation and refer the question to HR.
•  Demonstrate updating at least one policy document: track its source ID/version, replace or invalidate stale indexed chunks, and show a query that reflects the new policy.
•  Keep the assistant read-only for policy Q&A: employee-specific private records require an authorized identity-aware workflow.
ACCEPTANCE CRITERION
Supported questions cite the correct policy. Unsupported questions do not produce invented policies or fabricated citations.

Failure path: If a vector-store query fails, the assistant must report the retrieval issue instead of offering an unsupported policy answer.

06   Core Workflow — Certificate Generation
WORKFLOW 5   |   HR-Approved Experience Certificates
An employee or HR officer requests an experience certificate. The system prepares a draft using approved employment records, but an authorized HR user must approve release.
•  Collect a request_id and employee_id from an internal request form.
•  Look up approved employee records and retrieve name, job title, department, start date, end date (or “Present” for a current employee), and employment status.
•  Consult the company’s certificate policy through RAG to support an eligibility summary.
•  Prepare a structured certificate draft and send it to an authorized HR reviewer for explicit approve/reject action, with reviewer identity and decision timestamp recorded.
•  On approval, copy a branded Google Docs template, replace placeholders, export the result as a PDF, and save it to restricted Drive storage.
•  Send only an authorized download link and update the request with approver, decision time, certificate_id, and output reference.
ACCEPTANCE CRITERION
A valid HR-approved request creates exactly one PDF with correct placeholders. Unknown, rejected, incomplete, unverified, or unapproved requests never release a certificate.

Failure path: Never fabricate tenure, titles, official stamps, signatures, or an employment history not present in records.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
The AI may draft text, but template filling, permission checks, status updates, PDF generation, and idempotency are better handled by standard automation logic.
07   Core Workflow — Monitoring & Reliability
WORKFLOW 6   |   Scheduled HR Reporting & Recovery
Alongside event-driven submissions, the system performs scheduled monitoring so HR can identify unfinished cases and safely recover from transient errors.
•  Run one daily Schedule Trigger to inspect Candidates, Employees, Documents, and Requests.
•  Produce an HR digest: new applications, interviews pending, onboarding blockers, pending certificate approvals, failed requests, and overdue cases.
•  Send the digest once to the authorized HR mailbox; only include links or minimal safe fields, not raw ID content.
•  Retry only transient and eligible failures, with a retry count, stop condition, and audit trail.
•  Demonstrate catch-up by backdating an unfinished demo request, running the scheduled check, and showing it is processed once; also prove that repeated triggers do not duplicate side effects.
ACCEPTANCE CRITERION
A scheduled run reports accurate demo counts, and a repeated request or retry does not create duplicate rows, approvals, PDFs, or email notifications.

Failure path: Permanent validation errors must be escalated rather than retried indefinitely.

End-to-end storyline for the final demo
•  Applicant submits an AI Engineer CV → extraction and HR review summary.
•  HR records a hire → system creates an onboarding checklist and flags a missing document.
•  An employee asks about a policy → RAG responds with a citation.
•  A pre-existing eligible employee requests a certificate → HR approves → PDF is generated.
•  HR receives a monitoring summary and sees one handled error or duplicate attempt.

Recommended stack (substitutions allowed)
n8n for orchestration; n8n Forms or Webhooks for intake; an LLM provider for specialist agents; OCR or multimodal vision for scanned data; Google Sheets for demo records; Google Drive for restricted files; Gmail for notifications; Google Docs for templates; a vector database such as Qdrant or Supabase for RAG.
Access and data-handling requirements
•  Separate applicant, employee, and HR access. Protect approval entry points, verify the acting reviewer before changing a status, and never treat a public Form field claiming “HR approved” as authorization.
•  Use mock employee identifiers rather than national ID numbers as database keys.
•  Exclude sensitive data and API credentials from prompts, logs, screenshots, exports, and repository commits.
•  Uploaded documents and retrieved knowledge are untrusted content; do not follow instructions found inside them.
•  Validate and escape generated fields before inserting them into documents or emails; never expose unrestricted file links, and record errors without storing private document contents.
IMPLEMENTATION LEVEL
Google Sheets is acceptable as a teaching database for fabricated records. It is not a sufficient production architecture for handling real HR identities, employee documents, and approvals without additional security and governance work.

Definition of done
•  All six mandatory workflows can be triggered and demonstrated with synthetic data; complete T01–T18 with documented actual results (or clearly disclose any failed test).
•  At least four properly separated agent roles cooperate via a documented handoff or orchestration design.
•  Every RAG answer is supported by an actual indexed reference with a source identifier, or the assistant transparently declines unsupported claims.
•  HR controls candidate stage changes and certificate approval; sensitive operations are never AI-authorized.
•  Repeated events, missing inputs, API failures, and OCR uncertainty have visible, safe outcomes.
•  Another team member or assessor can import the workflows and reproduce the demo from the supplied README.
EVIDENCE STANDARD
For each test, record the input, expected output, actual result, and a screenshot or sanitized execution log. A successful green execution alone is not sufficient evidence that the business rule worked.

13   Optional Extensions & Final Guidance
The following extensions are optional and are not part of the core 100-point score. They may strengthen the final showcase but cannot replace core requirements. Attempt them only after all six workflows and T01–T18 have been completed.
BONUS A  |  Interview Coordination
•  After HR moves a candidate to Interview, propose a meeting time through an HR-controlled form.
•  Create a calendar invitation only after an authorized reviewer confirms the schedule.
•  Collect interview notes and produce a concise summary; do not automatically decide hiring outcomes.
BONUS B  |  Leave Request & Approval
•  Employee submits a leave request; retrieve relevant rules using RAG and check a fabricated leave-balance record.
•  Route to the responsible manager or HR reviewer for a real approval/rejection decision.
•  Update leave records and notifications without double-debiting balances on retries.
BONUS C  |  Multiple HR Document Templates
•  Extend the certificate workflow to an employment verification letter or a synthetic salary letter.
•  Choose the correct reusable template by request type, populate only verified fields, and require authorized approval.
•  Use unique document references, secure links, and revision-safe templates.
What not to build
Do not spend your core project time on payroll, biometric authentication, social media recruiting, a complex UI, real-world identity verification, or autonomous employment decisions. The objective is to demonstrate reliable, explainable AI automation with working business handoffs.
FINAL SUCCESS STANDARD
An excellent project is a connected HR workflow system that works with realistic synthetic inputs, keeps AI inside clearly defined boundaries, cites its policy sources, handles errors safely, and can be demonstrated from end to end—not merely a large collection of nodes.
